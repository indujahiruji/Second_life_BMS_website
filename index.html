What is a Second-Life Battery?

When a hybrid vehicle battery can no longer deliver the performance required for automotive use — typically when its capacity drops below 70–80% of original — it is removed from the vehicle and considered end-of-life for transport.

However, these batteries are far from useless. They still retain significant energy capacity perfectly suited for stationary energy storage applications such as backup power, solar integration, and grid buffering.

Second-life battery repurposing is the process of collecting these retired automotive cells, testing them, repackaging them, and deploying them in a new application — extending their useful life by 5 to 10 additional years while dramatically reducing electronic waste.

"Repurposing a single hybrid vehicle battery pack prevents approximately 80 kg of hazardous battery waste from entering landfill — while providing years of reliable backup power."

Why NiMH for This Project?

Nickel Metal Hydride (NiMH) batteries were the dominant chemistry in first-generation hybrid vehicles such as the Toyota Prius, Honda Insight, and similar models. Sri Lanka has a large fleet of these imported hybrid vehicles, creating a steady, local supply of retired NiMH packs.

NiMH cells offer excellent thermal stability, tolerate partial state-of-charge cycling well, and are significantly safer than lithium-ion chemistries — making them ideal for a university lab environment where safety is paramount.

The challenge, however, is that each retired pack contains modules with varying degrees of degradation. An intelligent BMS is essential to safely manage this cell-to-cell variation and extract maximum usable energy from the heterogeneous pack.

Six reasons second-life BMS technology is important for Sri Lanka and beyond.

⚠️ Challenges of Second-Life Batteries

📉
Cell-to-Cell Capacity Variation
Each module has a different State of Health (SoH), ranging from 35% to 83% in this pack. Managing 84 modules with varying capacity is the core BMS challenge.
⚡
Voltage Imbalance Between Strings
Weaker modules in a string limit the whole string's usable capacity. Without balancing, stronger modules are under-utilised while weak ones are over-stressed.
🌡️
Thermal Hotspots
Degraded cells generate more heat under the same current. Hotspots can accelerate degradation and in extreme cases cause thermal runaway.
❓
Unknown Charge History
Sourced from used vehicles, the exact cycle count and degradation history of each module is unknown, requiring on-site characterisation before deployment.

✅ How This BMS Solves Them

📊
Per-Module SoH Tracking
Each of the 84 modules is individually characterised and assigned an SoH value. The BMS uses this to predict remaining capacity and flag modules below 40% for retirement.
⚖️
Passive Cell Balancing
Above 85% SoC, passive balancing bleeds excess charge from stronger modules, equalising voltage across all 12 strings and maximising usable pack capacity.
🌡️
Real-Time Thermal Monitoring
Hottest module temperature is tracked continuously. Charge is inhibited at 45°C and the BMS trips the pack offline at 55°C — preventing any thermal damage.
🔬
On-Site Characterisation Protocol
Every module undergoes a controlled charge-discharge test before installation. Terminal voltage, internal resistance, and capacity are measured to build the SoH profile.

Protection & Safety Features

Multi-layer protection ensuring safe operation of second-life cells.

🔋
Over-Voltage Protection
Module OV trip at 9.60 V. Prevents cell damage from overcharge. Triggers charge cutoff immediately.
⬇️
Under-Voltage Protection
Module UV trip at 6.30 V. Hard BMS cutoff prevents deep discharge damage to second-life cells.
🌡️
Thermal Management
Charge inhibit at 45 °C. Full BMS trip at 55 °C. Hottest module temperature monitored in real time.
⚖️
Passive Cell Balancing
Activates above 85% SoC to equalise module voltages across all 12 strings, extending pack life.
📉
Smart Load Shedding
Two-level shed at 30% and 22% SoC prioritises critical lab equipment, maximising backup duration.
〰️
IEEE 519 THD Monitoring
Continuous THD monitoring with 5% limit flag. H3 (150 Hz), H5 (250 Hz), H7 (350 Hz) tracked independently.
🔌
−ΔV Termination
Detects the voltage dip at full charge characteristic of NiMH cells, preventing overcharge efficiently.
📊
SoH Retirement Tracking
Modules with SoH below 40% flagged for retirement. Real-time Remaining Useful Life estimation.
🔄
Float Charge (Grid-Tied)
Always connected to CEB grid. Float charge mode keeps pack ready for instant backup with top-off at 0.65 A.
